The opening, as a game

Your three steps, in the order a child meets them: a cover that promises a game, an inside cover that says what the game is, then the map of the world as the first spread, with places and creatures but no path yet. All words and names are placeholders for you to change.

3 · The first spread: the map

Like the map at the front of a fantasy book: the places and their creatures, with no path between them. The sea is on the left page and the land on the right, so the world reads from the oldest life to the newest. The route is drawn in later, as the child travels.

What's a placeholder:
  • All words: the title, the tagline, the promises, the place names.
  • The places and creatures: only the jellies (for Vehicle 1) are decided. The woodlouse, roundworm and ants come from your notes. The sea hare is the classic animal for habituation, and the moths, bees and frog are guesses for later stops. The map shows places, not stops: one place could host several stops.
  • The numbers in the promises, which follow the tentative eleven robots.
All the drawings are mine, made for this mock-up. The map has no "you are here" and no route yet; the guide could draw the route in red, stop by stop, and add each place's sticker as it's reached.
