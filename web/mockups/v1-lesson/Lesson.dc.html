Where it's bright, Val goes fast, so it doesn't stay long. Where it's dim, Val goes slowly, so it stays a long time. Leave it running and it ends up in the dim, as if it hated the light. Nothing inside Val avoids, loves or fears anything.
The woodlouse works the same way: more light, faster. It isn't afraid of anything; light just makes it hurry, and that keeps it out of the sun where it would dry out. FOR THE ADULT
Braitenberg's point: it's easier to build behaviour than to work out how it's built from watching. The child's word (love, fear, curiosity) describes the outside; the wiring is one line. Why woodlice do it: they lose water easily, and light usually means sun and dry air, so hurrying through bright places keeps them where it's damp.
